# Interview guide

## Questions about the past

Pick at most five. Replace "the problem" with the bet's problem.

- Tell me about the last time the problem happened. What did you do?
- What did it cost you: time, money, or customers?
- What do you use for it today? What do you pay for it?
- What did you try before that, and why did you stop?
- Who else in your company deals with it?
- How did you find out about the problem the last time?

## Questions to avoid

- "Would you use ...?" The answer is a guess about the future.
- "Do you like ...?" The answer is politeness.
- "How much would you pay?" Ask what they pay today.
- Any question that names your product before the person names the problem.

## Commitment ladder

End each conversation with an ask from the highest step that fits. Log the step that the person took, not the step that you asked for.

| Step | The person | Kind in the record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Gives more time: a second call, a demo | `interview` |
| 2 | Introduces you to a colleague or a person with the same problem | `interview` |
| 3 | Uses the product with their own data | `usage` |
| 4 | Signs a letter of intent or agrees a price in writing | `commitment` |
| 5 | Pays | `payment` |

A person who praises the product and takes no step gives no evidence. Write it in the notes, and do not add a row.
